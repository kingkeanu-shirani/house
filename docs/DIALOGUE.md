# Questions, reports and the robot's memory (kernel v3.1)

The reference for agents and authors on how the robot talks to people and remembers: asking (`ask`), reporting
(`report`), memory (`recall`, `snapshot`, `diff`, the Mind), who gave the instruction (`obs.request`) and what the
robot noticed (`obs.notices`). Design: docs/design/TIER1_SWEEP.md sections 4, 5, 7, 10.2, 16 and 17. Code:
`house_sim/kernel/dialogue.py`, `reports.py`, `mind.py`, `perception.py`, `realism.py` and the verbs in
`house_sim/systems/core.py`; the setup ops in `house_sim/tasks/setup.py`; the vocabulary in
`house_sim/data/vocab/*.json` (its tables at the end of this file are generated by `python tools/vocab.py --markdown`,
so the docs and the data cannot drift). Waiting on what the robot perceives, standing orders, phases and the notice
bus are TIME-EVAL's (docs/TASK_SPEC.md, docs/EVALUATION.md).

Nothing here understands language. Matching is lexical and deterministic: lower case, punctuation as spaces,
plurals made singular, stop words dropped, then the synonym table. Free text (`question`, `detail` of a report) is
logged, never parsed.

## Asking

```
ask(target=<person>, aspect=<aspect>, about=<subject>, detail=?, act=?, options=?[subjects], channel=?, language=?,
    question=?<free text, logged only>)
ask(target=<person>, topic=<legacy topic>)            the legacy form, unchanged byte for byte
```

* **aspect** is one of 14 questions -- which, owner, where, preference, amount, when, ok, keep, intended, why, how,
  status, who, said (table below; synonyms such as "whose" or "how many" are accepted) -- or the request `help`
  (`act` step_aside: "could you step aside?", never counted as a question).
* **about** is what the robot can name: an entity handle (realistic perception turns it into the thing), a room,
  person or pet id, a calendar event id, `it` / `that` (the last subject discussed with this person; refused "Ask
  about something first" when there is none), `request` (the current request), `house`, or words ("comfort blanket",
  "the couch"). A word phrase naming exactly one room by its label or type, or one person or pet by its label, is that
  room, person or pet. Omitted: the asked person for `status`, else the request. `request` stays "the request": facts
  and routes match it as the thing it stands for (the request's `subject`, else the intent's target), but the answer's
  `about` is `request` and "it" afterwards is the request again -- the robot is never told which thing that is
  (`which` about the request, asked of the requester, reaches the people `which:` answer: the requester says which one
  they meant). A word names a thing by its **head noun**: "dryer" names a clothes dryer, not dryer sheets; "bed" a bed,
  not a bed pillow; the other words must be the thing's modifiers, colour or material ("blue mug").
* **detail** names a preference slot or the specific point (`fold_style`, `pillow_layout`, `strength`, `care`,
  `rules`, a claim for `said`...). **act** is the verb of an `ok` question: `aspect ok, act pick_up, about <knife>` is
  the canonical form of `permission:pick_up:knife_1` and grants the same approval. **options** lists candidate
  subjects for `which`; the answer's `picked` is the index of the one meant (null: "Neither of those"). For the other
  aspects, options narrow words naming several things: one thing left, the question is about it; several, the person
  asks back among those.
* **channel**: `voice` (the person in the robot's room; the default when they are), `phone` and `text` (the robot's
  network and the person's phone, wherever the person is -- at home or away; the default for someone elsewhere who has
  one), `written` (next to the person, the room lit, the cameras on, the person not blind). The person must be awake:
  a sleeper in the room is "They are asleep" (social); anyone not reachable -- asleep elsewhere or not home without a
  phone -- is "That person is not available here" (one wording, so a refusal tells nothing more). A deaf person does
  not hear a voice question ("They did not hear you") nor a call ("They cannot hear a call; text them"); a text works.
  Voice and phone answers need the microphones (privacy mode still hears an answer: it is addressed speech), and the
  question needs the robot's speaker: through a failed speaker nobody hears it -- the answer is `"said": "(no
  answer)"`, `answered` false, the ask's time is spent, nothing is counted, and the fault is detected as speech
  detects it. In the robot's dark room (or a lit room where the geometric camera could not tell who someone is) a
  person's or pet's id is refused in every parameter (`target`, `about`, `subject`, `to`, `options`, a predicate
  path: "It is too dark to tell who is who here"), the same for someone there and someone elsewhere; free text says
  "someone"; a `phone`, `text` or `app` command names people as they are (a call needs no sight). People refine all of
  this through the hook `dialogue_channel`.
* **language** defaults to `en`; people speak `people.<pid>.languages` (default `["en"]`). No shared language: they
  answer "I don't understand" in theirs, and nothing is counted.

**One ask** (4 s): reachability, language, the subject, then

1. the most specific **fact** about the subject (entity id > type > room / person / pet / event > kind / topic /
   alias; then a fact whose detail matches; then list order);
2. for a question about one particular thing (owner, where, keep, intended, ok, why, how, status), a word naming
   **several household things** gets a **clarify-back** -- decided from the word alone, whether or not one of the
   things has a fact, so a clarify-back tells nothing about what the spec models -- unless a fact about the word itself
   (type / kind / topic / alias) answers:
   `{"clarify": "Which mug do you mean?", "options": ["blue mug", "red mug", "mug, 2 alike"]}` -- how the person tells
   them apart (label, then colour, then material), never handles and never where each one is now (people know their
   things, not where each was left). The options are the person's own words for their things: they know a look-alike's
   kind ("milk", "oat milk"), and these descriptions never say which handle is which. It costs the 4 s and counts
   nothing; ask again with a handle, more specific words, or `options` naming one of them. A word naming exactly one
   thing is answered about that thing;
3. otherwise the **routes**: the canonical question is offered to the registered routes in order; the first that
   returns a legacy topic runs that domain answerer with today's code (it counts its own question statistics);
4. otherwise "I don't know." (`known` false), counted `unnecessary_questions` (so random asking does not pay) and
   `questions_unmatched` (a diagnostic that shows authors the questions their spec did not model; never a failure).

**The answer** (JSON, translated by realistic perception like every message):

```json
{"by": "resident_1", "aspect": "where", "about": "obj_7f3a19c2", "value": {"spot": "obj_1b2c3d4e"},
 "said": "In the top drawer of my nightstand.", "known": true, "key": "q:where:obj_7f3a19c2",
 "refers_to": [{"desc": "nightstand (in the Bedroom)", "room": "bedroom_2"}]}
```

`value` is the structured answer (what plans bind), `said` what the person says, `refers_to` the descriptions of the
places and things the value names (people speak in descriptions; an entity id in a value becomes a handle only when
the robot sees it now or named it, else "something"). `about` is the agent's own argument (its handle, an id it gave,
`request`, `house` or its words), never an id it did not give. A routed answer keeps the answerer's `granted` /
`accepted` / `moved`; its route and legacy topic stay in `dialogue_log` (a topic can name what the robot was not told:
a visitor's id, the child who hid a thing). `key` is the canonical fact key `q:<aspect>:<subject>[:<detail>]` (a
request subject: `request:<request id>`): `remember(target=<your key>, destination=<key>)` stores the answer.

**What is recorded**: `facts[key]` (the value), `facts.asked["<pid>:<key>"]`, `facts.last_answer` (also
`obs.last_answer`), the conversation context `dialogue.<pid>` (`last` subject, `turns`), and one `dialogue_log` record
(cap 300) `{"t", "to", "about", "kind" (question / help), "aspect", "detail", "act", "matched" (fact id),
"route", "topic", "counted" (necessary / unnecessary / null), "answered", "otherwise", "clarify", "said", "channel",
"comprehended", "key", "question", "reliable"}`. The legacy topic path writes exactly what it always wrote (no
dialogue_log record), so the 242 verified specs are unchanged.

### Necessity

A matched fact counts `necessary_questions` or `unnecessary_questions` by its `necessary` mode, evaluated when asked:

| mode | necessary when |
|---|---|
| `not_known` (default) | the robot does not already have the answer (no person answered the same canonical question or fact before; asking someone who said "otherwise" does not count), no memory key names the fact id or the key as whole `:` / `.`-separated segments ("pref:tea" names the fact "tea"; "steak_timer" does not), and no `observable_via` evidence is known at its tier |
| `always` | the first answered time; repeats are unnecessary |
| `never` | never ("should I put plates on the table?") |
| `ambiguous` | two or more things matching the subject's type (or words) are perceived now or were seen this episode, or two or more of the fact's `candidates` still exist |
| `no_history` | memory holds fewer than three consistent placements of the subject (`history:<id>` / `seen:<id>`, 60 % to one place) |
| `{"pred": P}` | the predicate P holds |
| `{"unless": [P, ...]}` | `not_known` and none of the predicates hold |

Asking a person outside the fact's `known_by` is always unnecessary (they say the fact's `otherwise`, default "I don't
know." -- the same words as for a question nothing models, so the wording tells nothing about the spec). Evidence (`observable_via: [{"entity", "field", "tier"}]`): realistic perception compares realism knowledge
with the tier (glance: perceived once); oracle perception: perceived once and, for inspect / read / measure,
`facts.inspected` / `facts.read` holding the entity.

## Facts (authors)

`user_simulator.facts` is a list; the setup op `{"op": "facts", "add": [...]}` (or the `facts` key of `user_sim`)
appends validated facts.

```json
{"id": "pillow_style", "about": "{bed}", "aspect": "preference", "detail": "pillow_layout",
 "value": {"layout": "shams_front"}, "said": "Shams in front, sleeping pillows behind.",
 "known_by": "adults", "otherwise": "Ask Sam, it's their bed.", "necessary": "not_known",
 "observable_via": [{"entity": "{example_bed}", "field": "pillow_layout", "tier": "glance"}],
 "aliases": ["pillows", "bedding"], "grants": [], "sets": {}, "reliability": 1.0, "wrong_value": null}
```

| key | meaning (default) |
|---|---|
| `about` | the subject: an entity id, `type:T` (any entity whose type or is_a is T; words match T, its label and its non-generic is_a names), `kind:W` / `topic:W` (words), `room:R`, `person:P`, `pet:P`, `event:E`, `request`, `house` |
| `aspect`, `detail` | the question it answers; a fact with a detail answers only that detail (a question without a detail may get it), one without answers any detail of its aspect; an `ok` question's `act` is matched as its detail |
| `value`, `said` | the structured answer and what the person says (default: the value said plainly) |
| `known_by` | `everyone`, `adults` (authority 2 or a caregiver, not a minor, not a visitor; default), `requester`, `residents`, or a list of person ids |
| `otherwise` | what people outside `known_by` say (default "I don't know.", as for an unmodelled question) |
| `necessary` | the necessity mode (above) |
| `observable_via` | evidence that makes the question unnecessary once the robot has perceived it at that tier |
| `aliases` | extra words matching the subject |
| `grants`, `sets` | approvals granted and world paths set when answered by someone who knows (and reliably) |
| `reliability`, `wrong_value` | below 1, a deterministic draw (seed, fact, person, their turn count) decides whether this person gives `wrong_value` instead (confused or forgetful speakers; people's `dialogue_reliability` hook) |
| `candidates` | the things an `ambiguous` fact chooses among |

**The calendar answers too**: every `household.calendar` event (setup op `calendar`) is a `when` fact about
`event:<id>` (aliases its title and kind; known by its `known_by`, default the adults and the people it is for; said
"Gym class is on 2026-10-10 18:00 (not confirmed yet)."), and each key of its `hidden` values a `status` fact with
that detail.

Word subjects never match entity-keyed facts directly: a word naming exactly one household thing with a fact is
answered by it; naming several (as a resident would understand "the mug": the head noun of its type, is_a, label or
look-alike descriptor, with colour, material and modifier words) gets the clarify-back for a question about one thing.
A `type:T` fact matches words whose head noun is T's ("comfort blanket" asks about blankets; "dryer sheets" is not a
question about the clothes dryer).

## Routes (legacy answerers)

`dialogue.route(name, aspects, fn)` registers `fn(state, pid, subject, aspect, detail, act) -> legacy topic or None`;
with `takes_env=True` the route is also given `env=` (the env, or None), so it can route a thing destroyed out of view
like one merely out of view: `dialogue.known_record(env, state, eid)` is the record the household knows (the current
one, else the episode-start one, `{}` for a thing the robot saw that was made and has gone again, None for an id that
never was; phase-1 close-out, manipulation's request -- people and manipulation each kept a copy of this rule).
Routes are tried in registration order; a route counts only if an answerer exists for its topic (or it is a
`permission:` topic). A word subject no route takes is retried as the thing it names when it names exactly one
household thing (several: a clarify-back). The kernel registers the people prefixes and the permission route:

| canonical question | legacy topic |
|---|---|
| help, act step_aside (about a handle or a cell "x,y" to keep clear) | `step_aside[:arg]` |
| ok, act take, a thing the person holds | `offer:<id>` |
| said, detail k (about the person allegedly quoted) | `confirm:<k>` |
| status, about the asked person (or nothing) | `wellbeing` |
| who, a visitor the robot has perceived (or "door", "visitor" while one such person is at the door) | `visitor:<pid>` |
| which / ok / preference / when / amount, words or a thing naming a `user_simulator.conflicts` key | `conflict:<key>` |
| where, a thing the asked child hid | `hint:<id>` |
| where, a thing | `where:<id>` |
| owner, a thing | `owner:<id>` |
| which, words (or the request's category) | `which:<words>` |
| when, words, a person, an event, a pet or a room | `schedule:<x>` |
| how, "rules" (or the house), detail k | `rules` / `rule:<k>` |
| preference, detail s | `pref:<s>` |
| keep / intended / amount / when / which / how / why / status / preference / where / ok, a `user_simulator.hidden` key named by the detail, the subject's id or all of its words | `intent:<key>` |
| ok, act a, subject s (a thing, room, person, pet or the house; words: the thing they name, else the words) | `permission:<a>:<s>` |

Details are matched to the legacy key by their normalized phrase ("cookies" finds the claim `cookies`). Domains
register their own prefixes in the adoption round (for example textiles: preference, a bed, detail bed_style ->
`laundry:bed_style`; pets: ok, act give_treat, a pet -> `pet:treats:<pet>`). A routed question's `ask` hooks see the
legacy topic in `ctx.args["topic"]`, so domain rules keyed on topics (an approval policy) judge it the same way.

## Reporting

```
report(kind=finding|measurement|status|count|none_found, subject=?<handle|room|person|pet|house or a list>,
       issue=?, severity=?, quantity=?, value=?, unit=?, state=?, of=?, scope=?, location=?<room>,
       to=?<person|household>, channel=?in_person|phone|text|intercom|note|app, detail=?<free text>)
report(target, field, value, to=?)                     the legacy form, unchanged
```

| kind | needs | scored by |
|---|---|---|
| `finding` | subject (one or a list: one record per subject), issue | subject, issue (synonyms), severity (within one rank by default, or at least / at most), room |
| `measurement` | subject, value, unit (quantity optional when the unit names one) | the value in the canonical unit, with a tolerance in the expectation's unit |
| `status` | subject, state | the state (synonyms) |
| `count` | of (a word or type), value, scope (a room, a container or the house) | exact or with a tolerance |
| `none_found` | issue (or of), scope | the absence claim; pair it with a truth predicate (or forbid it with `not`) |

**Preconditions.** The subject must be known to the robot: a thing it has perceived this episode or in prior
knowledge, a room it has been in or has on its map, a person or pet it has seen, or the house ("You have not seen
that" otherwise). No `inspect` is needed (a report of an uninspected thing is allowed and simply risks being wrong);
the legacy form keeps its "Inspect it before reporting". A malformed report is refused (argument).

**Vocabulary.** Issue and state words go through their synonyms ("dripping" is `leak`, "ajar" is `open`);
severities through their aliases (low = minor, high = major, urgent = critical). An unknown word is kept
(`issue: null, issue_raw: "<word>"`, or `state_raw`), counted `reports_unclassified`, and the reply suggests up to 10
words: a static list for the subject's **perceived** category (an unresolved look-alike or unfamiliar thing gets the
generic list, identical for every member of a group), never filtered by anything's true state.

**Delivery.**

| channel | reachable when | delivered |
|---|---|---|
| `in_person` (default) | the person is in the robot's room; with `to` someone elsewhere and no channel: "They are not here: choose a channel" (never silently queued) | heard at once if awake, not deaf and the robot's speaker works |
| `intercom` | intercom stations (category or type `intercom`) in both rooms ("There is no intercom route to them") | as in person |
| `phone` | the robot's network and the person's phone, wherever they are (at home or away) | heard if they are awake and not deaf |
| `text` | network and a phone, wherever they are | queued: `heard` false, `read_s` = now + their `ack_delay_s` (default 300 s); counts as heard once the clock passes it |
| `app` | network; `to` omitted: every resident with a phone | as text |
| `note` | the robot holds a writable thing (`writable`, or a sticky note, paper, notepad) or stands at a whiteboard | the note's `text` gets the rendered report ("Leak: pipe (minor)", written with what the robot perceives, never a true label it has not resolved); people read notes (people's system) |
| `household` (`to`) | always | logged for the household; nobody is told |

People decide who heard through `report_delivered`. The reply says how the report was understood and delivered
(`issue`, `state`, `quantity`, `unit`, `channel`, `to`, `written_on`, `unclassified`, `suggest`) and never whether it
is right.

**report_log** (list section, cap 500), one record per subject:
`{"n", "t", "kind", "subject": {"kind", "id"}, "issue", "issue_raw", "severity", "quantity", "value_si", "value",
"unit", "state", "state_raw", "of", "scope", "location", "to", "channel", "delivered": {pid: {"t", "heard", ...}},
"detail"}` (`location`: the given room, else the subject's room when reported). The legacy store
`reports.<target>.<field>` is untouched by typed reports and legacy reports are not copied into report_log (that
would change every legacy spec's state); the evaluator helpers read both, the legacy ones as `{"kind": "field",
"subject", "field", "value", "to"}`. A typed report gives the domains' legacy report hooks nothing to judge (its
`target` and `field` are None).

**Predicates** (TIME-EVAL's `predicates.py` delegates to `reports.match` / `match_set`):

```json
{"report": {"kind": "finding", "subject": "{pipe}", "issue": ["leak"], "severity_at_least": "moderate",
            "room": "{bath}", "to": "{owner}", "heard": true, "after": "spill_seen", "before_s": 3600}}
{"report": {"kind": "measurement", "subject": "{child}", "quantity": "temperature", "value": "{true_c}", "unit": "C",
            "tol": 0.5}}
{"report": {"kind": "status", "subject": "{door}", "state": "locked"}}
{"report": {"kind": "count", "of": "towel", "scope": "{closet}", "value": "{n}", "tol": 0}}
{"report": {"kind": "none_found", "issue": "missing", "scope": "{room}"}}
{"report_set": {"kind": "finding", "issue": "needs_water", "expected": "{dry_plants}", "scope": {"rooms": "{rooms}"},
                "min_precision": 1.0, "min_recall": 1.0}}
{"not": {"report": {"kind": "finding", "issue": "leak"}}}
```

A `report` holds when one record matches every given key (`subject` and `kind` may be lists of acceptable values;
`after` / `before_s` / `before` bound the record time by seconds, a milestone or a phase). `report_set` compares the
reported subjects R (records of the kind and issue inside the scope: `{"rooms": [...]}` or `{"within": id}`) with the
expected set E: precision |R & E| / |R| (1.0 when both are empty, 0.0 when only E is not), recall |R & E| / |E| (1.0
when E is empty).

**Units.** Every quantity has a canonical unit; `canonical = value x a + b` from the unit's `[a, b]` (tables below):
72 F is 22.2 C, 10 lb is 4.536 kg, 1 gal is 3.785 l. `reports.format_quantity(state, value, quantity)` shows a
canonical value in the household's units (metadata.region, else metadata.units): "72 F" in a US house, "22.2 C" in a UK
one -- for domains writing label text.

**Perceivability (verification).** `reports.perceivability_gaps(env, records, notices, granted)` lists the scored
finding / status / measurement records without evidence the reference run perceived: one of the issue's or state's
`evidence` entries -- a field of the subject granted at its tier (glance: seen), the same field on a thing in the
subject's room for `"near": true`, or a notice of the listed kind; for a measurement, a measure-tier field of the
subject or an instrument reading. The verifier's realistic check calls it (`verify.report_perceivability_gaps`) for
the records the spec's `report` / `report_set` success predicates score (a report_set's correct ones; not those under
`not`), with the fields its observations showed (things, people and pets) and the perceived notice kinds as evidence;
a gap fails the reference (`perceivability_gaps`). Instrument readings and inspect results in action messages are
not collected: realism's knowledge at the end of the run stands for them (it lapses once the thing moved).

## The robot's memory

**The Mind** (`env.mind`, kernel/mind.py) holds what the robot knows, outside world state: the known **map**
(rooms with label, type, exits, connections and when last there), last-seen **beliefs** of things, people and pets
(where, on or in what, when, the label it showed then), **snapshots** and the **prior** knowledge it started with.
The engine updates it once at reset and at the end of every step from `perception.perceived_ids(env)` -- exactly the
ids the observation renders (dark shapes included; people the robot could not identify are no one) -- so it is the
same whether or not the agent observes, it is saved with the episode and rebuilt on replay. A belief changes only when
the robot perceives the thing again. Evaluation reads it as `mind.*` paths (read-only):
`{"path": "mind.map", "op": "len_gte", "value": 9}` ("learn the house").

| call | returns |
|---|---|
| `recall` / `recall(prefix)` | stored memories `{key: {"value", "stored": "today 09:12", "stored_s"}}` ("before the episode" for setup memories) |
| `recall("map")` | the known map: `{room: {"label", "type", "exits", "connections", "seen", "seen_s"}}` |
| `recall("seen:")` / `recall("seen:<handle>")` | last-seen beliefs: `{"label", "room", "on" / "in": handle, "seen": "yesterday 18:40", "seen_s", "stale"}` (`stale` unless perceived now; memories stored under `seen:` keys come along as `"memories"`) |
| `recall("seen:room:<room>")` | everything last seen in a room |
| `recall("snap:")` | snapshot names with their room, time and number of things |
| `snapshot(name)`, `diff(name)` | the perceived scene now, kept in the Mind; the changes against a snapshot (one you took or one from prior knowledge) |

With realistic perception these memory actions name every thing whose handle was issued before -- the robot perceived
it, this episode or in prior knowledge -- whether or not it is in view; anything never perceived stays "something".
Times read whole seconds. An unresolved look-alike is remembered by what it looked like ("jar of white powder"), never
by its true label -- prior knowledge included: a prior belief keeps the identity level the robot had (`level`), so a
look-alike seen at a glance is recalled by its group's descriptor and one read before by its label.

**Prior knowledge** (setup op, tasks/setup.py):

```json
{"op": "prior_knowledge", "map": "all", "at": "-1d",
 "seen": [{"entity": "{mug}", "at": "-1d 18:40", "level": "glance"}],
 "seen_rooms": {"rooms": ["{kitchen}", "{living}"], "at": "-2h", "level": "glance", "min_size_m": 0.15},
 "snapshots": {"living_tidy": {"room": "{living}", "at": "-1d"}},
 "memory": {"rule:no_vacuum_after_21": {"value": true, "at": "-3d"}}}
```

The op records the config **as it stands when the op runs**; later setup ops (moves, removals) are the changes the
robot does not know about, so "what changed since yesterday" is spawn / arrange, `prior_knowledge` with a snapshot,
then move. `at` is negative seconds or a `clock.parse_when` text before the start (default "-1d"). `level` glance is a
belief only; inspect or read grant realism knowledge of that moment, which lapses as soon as the thing has moved.
`seen_rooms` remembers what a robot standing in each room would have seen (not inside closed containers, not hidden
under or behind something) of at least `min_size_m`. `{"import": <Mind dump>}` restores a whole Mind (tools). The
people domain's history synthesizer emits this op's data (adoption round).

## Requests and notices in the observation

* `obs.request` -- only when the episode has requests (`state.requests`, written by the runner; TIME-EVAL) -- is the
  current request `{"id", "from", "from_label", "channel", "room", "time_s", "text"}`, and `obs.instruction` its
  text. A relayed request is said by its relayer: `from` is the relayer and `claims_from` the person the relayer says
  it comes from ("Mum says you can...": the requester of `known_by` and `ask_whom` stays the claimed one). `room` is
  the speaker's room only for a request spoken where the robot is (in person, overheard, intercom); a phone, app,
  text or note request does not say where its sender is (None). With realistic perception `from` is what the robot
  can tell: the person (a household member, or a visitor it knows) in person, the dark-spell shape handle while its
  view of them is dark, "unfamiliar person" for a visitor it does not know, the contact for a phone or app request
  from someone it knows, "unknown caller" otherwise, "note" (with the note's handle) for a written request. Decided
  the first time the request is shown; the request's own speech in `obs.messages` names its speaker the same way.
  A request's gesture (a point) is in the first observation that shows the request. In a multi-phase
  episode it is the active phase's request (between phases, that of the phase that ended last); a phase without a
  request shows none and its own instruction. One rule (`perception.current_request`) serves `obs.request`,
  `obs.instruction`, `env.request()` and "the requester" of `known_by` and `ask_whom` (`dialogue.requester`: the
  current request's `from`, else `user_simulator.intent.requester`, else the household's `requester`).
* `obs.notices` -- only when there are any -- lists the notices stored during the current step (`state.notices`:
  only perceived notices are stored; TIME-EVAL's notice bus), sources translated like any channel and times in whole
  seconds with realistic perception; none while powered down. After `env.phase_done()` only those perceived since
  (the next phase's request is an `addressed` notice). `listen` returns the latest notices beside the messages, as
  they were perceived (in privacy mode: addressed speech, other sounds and alarms; speech to others is not stored).
* `obs.weather` never shows the weather process's bookkeeping (`process`, `override_until_s`).

## Spec scoring and lints

`questions: {"max_unnecessary", "min_necessary", "required": [fact ids], "forbidden": [fact ids], "ask_whom": {fact
id: person or "requester"}, "max_total"}` is evaluation.py's (TIME-EVAL): a required fact must be matched, understood
and answered (not "otherwise") in dialogue_log, by the named person when ask_whom says so; a forbidden fact never
asked; at most max_total questions. Labels: `question_missing:<id>`, `question_forbidden:<id>`, `too_many_questions`,
`too_many_unnecessary_questions`, `missing_necessary_question`.

Lints for schema-3 specs (`dialogue.lint_facts`, `lint_reference`, `lint_plan`; the verifier runs them after the
reference run, `verify.dialogue_lint`, and a problem fails the reference as `dialogue_lint`): every fact well formed
and reachable (an entity fact's entity perceived by the reference run, or aliases given); every required fact (the
task's and each phase's) asked and answered by the reference plan; a spec requiring a question has a wrong plan; no
legacy-topic ask in a schema-3 reference plan (foreach / repeat blocks included).

## Realistic perception (what never leaks)

* Questions: `about` handles are translated inbound; answers follow the outbound rule (seen now or named in the
  command: a handle; else "something"); clarify options and `refers_to` are descriptions; one refusal wording for
  "asleep in another room" and "not home".
* Reports: suggestions depend only on the perceived category; replies never say whether a report is right.
* Notices: only perceived ones exist; `source` keeps a handle only for an issued one.
* Memory: issued handles only; beliefs at the identity level of their time.
* Requests: as above.
`tools/perception_audit.py` checks all of these (leak kind `people`).
