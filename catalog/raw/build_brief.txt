WHAT "INCORPORATED" MEANS
Give every entry in both catalogs a stable ID, keep its original text verbatim, and track it in coverage.csv. An entry is done only when all of these hold:
1. World: every object, appliance, surface, room, person, animal, and condition it needs exists in the world model with the properties it depends on.
2. Actions: it can be accomplished through the action interface below.
3. Spec: a machine-readable task spec exists with a randomized initial-state generator, several natural-language phrasings of the command, a success predicate, failure and side-effect predicates, a risk level, and a step/time budget.
4. Verification: a scripted reference solution (at the entry's fidelity level) passes; a do-nothing baseline fails; and, where relevant, a plausible-but-wrong baseline fails (e.g. throws away the important item, or "cleans" by moving the mess elsewhere).
5. Record: coverage.csv shows its status (TODO, STUB, DONE, BLOCKED) and fidelity.

Fidelity levels: S = symbolic state and predicates; G = geometric (collision, reachability, containment, stacking, clearance); P = full physics (contact, deformables, liquids, thermal). Every entry must reach at least S with verification. Raise fidelity where it changes what the skill requires (pouring, folding, cutting, doors, stairs, carrying, untangling). If something truly cannot be represented, mark it BLOCKED with a concrete reason and the nearest representable proxy. Never silently skip, merge away, or delete an entry because it is hard.

WHAT TO BUILD
- World: procedural homes (apartment to multi-story house, plus yard, garage, shed, pool, driveway, curb, mailbox) with varied layouts, doors and handles, floors, lighting, time of day, season, and weather. Appliances and fixtures are working state machines (stove, oven, microwave, fridge, dishwasher, washer, dryer, HVAC, irrigation, locks, windows, blinds, lights, alarms, water/gas/power with shutoffs and outages).
- Objects: category, material, mass, size, fragility, temperature, wetness, cleanliness, contents, ownership, where it belongs, freshness, value (including sentimental), hazard flags, and affordances. Include consumables and inventory, waste streams, plants with care needs, and hazards (spills, leaks, smoke, broken glass, pests).
- People and animals: residents, guests, visitors, and pets with schedules, preferences, ownership, authority (adult/child/guest), privacy zones, moods, and abilities. They speak, gesture, point, interrupt, change their minds, give conflicting, vague, or relative instructions, and correct the robot. A user simulator holds the hidden ground truth behind ambiguous commands and answers clarifying questions; score both unnecessary and missing questions.
- Memory, time, and events: household state persists across episodes. Support snapshots and diffs ("put it back exactly how it was"), who-moved-what logs, routines, standing orders, conditional triggers, deferred tasks, and interruption with resumption. Add scripted and stochastic perturbations (spills, leaks, alarms, outages, people or pets entering the path, doors closing, objects moved or missing, weather changes).
- Action interface, three layers: (1) the catalog's primitives plus Speak, Listen, Announce, Ask, Refuse, Undo, Stop, Resume, and Hand off to a human; (2) parameterized skills composed from them; (3) a natural-language command channel with pointing and gaze references. Provide reset/step, headless mode, deterministic seeds, replay logging, introspectable preconditions and effects, and affordance queries ("what can be done with this object right now?"). Observations are configurable (egocentric RGB-D, proprioception, force/tactile, audio, language), with an optional privileged symbolic state for oracles and debugging.
- Safety and norms: hazard detection; risk levels 0 to 3 assigned per task and context (ladders, power tools, hot oil, medication, infant care, bathing, and lifting or transferring people sit at the top and require human approval or presence); privacy rules; consequences expressed as failure conditions and penalties (breakage, fire, flood, injury flags), never graphic depiction. Model intimate-care and medical tasks abstractly (state changes plus consent and supervision requirements), not as detailed body simulation.
- Evaluation: success, side effects ("don't make it worse"), safety and privacy violations, efficiency, quietness and social fit, quality of clarifying questions, reversibility. For subjective tasks ("make this place look nice"), combine a fixed rubric, programmatic proxies, and, if available, an LLM judge with a documented prompt calibrated against known-good and known-bad cases.
- Variation: randomize across every axis in "Situational variations," plus apartments vs. houses (elevators, shared laundry), regional standards (metric/imperial, appliance styles, shoes-off homes), languages and accents, residents with disabilities, and robot-side degradation (dirty camera, low battery, network loss, partial actuator failure).
- Extensibility: adding a task, object, appliance, or command is a data-only change (a spec file plus an optional predicate plugin) with no engine edits. Group near-identical entries into parameterized families, but keep a mapping from every catalog ID to its family and parameters so coverage is counted per entry.
- Stack: if the existing spec names an engine or language, use it. Otherwise pick a mature option and justify it in DECISIONS.md, allowing hybrid fidelity (symbolic fallbacks for liquids, cloth, knots) where the engine falls short. Prefer declarative, predicate-based task definitions (initial and goal conditions) and procedural house generation, as in existing household-robotics benchmarks. Do not copy unlicensed code or assets; record asset licenses.
